// about

Writing for people who build things

Stackframe started from a simple frustration: too much technology writing is either recycled press releases or padded-out filler written to satisfy a word count. We wanted something else — short of nowhere to hide, long enough to be useful.

What we do

Stackframe publishes original articles on web development, artificial intelligence, and the practical, unglamorous craft of programming. Every piece is written from scratch, reviewed for accuracy, and edited for clarity before it goes live. We would rather publish ten honest, useful articles than a hundred thin ones.

We write for a broad audience of people who work with technology in some form — developers, students, and anyone curious enough to want a clear explanation instead of a marketing pitch. Where a topic is genuinely complicated, we try to say so, rather than flattening it into something falsely simple.

How we work

Every article on Stackframe follows the same basic process: a clear angle, a first draft grounded in real explanation rather than filler, and an editorial pass focused on cutting anything that doesn't earn its place. We link to related articles when it's genuinely useful for the reader, not as a habit, and we avoid recycling the same idea across multiple posts just to pad out the archive.

// values

What we care about

Clarity

Plain language over jargon. If a concept needs an unfamiliar term, we explain it the first time it appears.

Originality

Every article is written specifically for Stackframe. Nothing here is recycled, spun, or auto-generated.

Usefulness

We ask whether an article actually helps a reader do or understand something, not just whether it fills space.

Who writes here

Articles on Stackframe are currently written and edited by Avery Chen, along with occasional contributions from guest writers with hands-on experience in the topics we cover. Author information is listed on every article.

Have a question, a correction, or a topic you'd like to see covered? Get in touch — we read every message.
